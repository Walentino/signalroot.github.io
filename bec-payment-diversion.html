Incident Investigation Report, Business Email Compromise Simulation

Incident ID: INC-ICS-2026-0718-002 Related incident: INC-ICS-2026-0716-001 (AiTM credential phishing). This incident is the continuation. AiTM was the entry, BEC is the objective. Classification: Controlled detection-validation exercise (lab, self-generated telemetry, not a real breach) Attack type: Business Email Compromise, FBI Type 3 (account compromise) with Type 1 characteristics (vendor invoice fraud / payment diversion) Environment: iCustomerSolutions M365 E5 tenant, Microsoft Sentinel workspace law-sentinel-lab, Defender XDR unified portal Compromised mailbox: dave@iCustomerSolutions.onmicrosoft.com Fraud target: alice@iCustomerSolutions.onmicrosoft.com Investigation date: 18 July 2026 Status: Closed. Evidence preserved, contained, eradicated. Times: UTC throughout (source events captured in ET, ET + 4h)

1. Executive Summary

On 18 July 2026, a controlled business email compromise exercise was performed against the mailbox compromised in INC-ICS-2026-0716-001, continuing the same simulated actor's activity. From an authenticated session the actor created a mail interception rule targeting the keywords "invoice" and "payment" and routing matches to Deleted Items, then sent a payment diversion email to an internal recipient requesting that banking details be changed on a $47,000 invoice, then deleted the sent item to suppress evidence. The full sequence took six minutes. Unlike the preceding AiTM incident, no infrastructure indicator was present: every action originated from the user's own residential IP address on a normal browser in a legitimately authenticated session, so no anomalous sign-in, no anonymised source, and no malicious content existed to detect. Evidence was preserved, the recipient was notified out of band, the interception rule and fraudulent message were removed, and sessions were revoked, all within 49 minutes of the fraudulent send. No payment was initiated and no financial loss occurred. The exercise validated behavioural BEC detection logic and demonstrated why infrastructure based detection fails against this attack class. Hardening recommendations are in Section 7.

Recoverability note. The user interface action was "permanently delete", but the audit layer recorded SoftDelete, meaning the item moved to Recoverable Items rather than being purged. A HardDelete would indicate purge from Recoverable Items. The distinction is operationally important: soft deleted evidence remains retrievable by an administrator, so the fraudulent message could have been recovered for a bank recall or law enforcement referral even after the actor believed it was destroyed.

4. Investigation Method

The correlation basis for this incident differs fundamentally from INC-ICS-2026-0716-001 and is the central finding of the exercise.

No infrastructure signal existed. Every event, the rule creation, the send, and both deletions, originated from 142.198.143.161, the user's established residential IP address in Ontario, on Client=OWA with an ordinary Mac Chrome user agent. This matches the user's normal baseline exactly. There was no risky sign-in, no anonymised source, no proxy indicator, and no Conditional Access anomaly. The IP pivot technique used in the preceding AiTM investigation would not have surfaced any of this activity.

Correlation was therefore behavioural and sequential. Three individually unremarkable events form a fraud pattern when read in order and in proximity:

  1. Creation of a rule that intercepts financial keywords and hides matches, which is not typical end user behaviour.
  2. A send containing financial change vocabulary in the subject line.
  3. Deletion of that sent item minutes later.

No single event warrants an alert. The sequence, compressed into six minutes, does. This is the detection principle for BEC: the signal is in the pattern and its timing, not in any indicator of compromise.

Queries used: OfficeActivity filtered on New-InboxRule / Set-InboxRule / UpdateInboxRules, on Send, and on HardDelete / SoftDelete / MoveToDeletedItems, each scoped to the affected mailbox. Rule intent was established by reading the Parameters field, which recorded the keyword conditions and destination folder in full.

5. Containment, Evidence Preservation, and Eradication

BEC response sequencing differs from AiTM response. Evidence is preserved before eradication, because mailbox artefacts and audit records may be required for bank recall or law enforcement referral. Removing the interception rule or the fraudulent message first can destroy proof that the fraud occurred.

Order Action Time (UTC) Verification
1 Preserve. Audit evidence captured and exported: full rule parameters, message subject, InternetMessageId, recipient, and both deletion events Prior to 14:25 Query result sets retained as the evidence package
2 Notify. Fraud target contacted out of band by voice, not email, so the potentially monitored mailbox was not used 14:25 Recipient confirmed no payment action taken
3 Eradicate persistence. Inbox rule "SS Sync" removed 14:26 Rules list re-checked, empty
4 Remove fraudulent message. Email deleted from recipient mailbox 14:27 Message no longer present. In production this would be executed centrally via Defender email purge rather than per mailbox
5 Contain identity. All refresh tokens and sessions revoked for dave 14:30 Session revocation completed

Rationale for session revocation. Unlike the preceding AiTM incident, where a stolen token was the live threat, the BEC activity here originated from a legitimately authenticated session. Revocation was performed anyway because the root cause is the unresolved identity compromise from INC-ICS-2026-0716-001, and session integrity could not be assumed. Where the underlying compromise is uncertain, revocation is the defensible choice.

Recipient notification method. Notification was made by voice rather than email deliberately. If the mailbox is under attacker observation, an emailed warning tips off the actor and may itself be intercepted by the rule under investigation.

6. Root Cause

The proximate cause is unremediated mailbox access from INC-ICS-2026-0716-001. The BEC email is the symptom. The identity compromise is the cause, and remediation that addresses only the fraudulent email leaves the underlying access intact.

Contributing factors that allowed the activity to proceed undetected by technical controls:

  • No detection on inbox rule creation. A rule combining financial keywords with a hide or delete action was created with no alert raised.
  • Email authentication provides no protection in this attack class. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all pass, because the message is genuinely sent from the legitimate mailbox on the legitimate domain. Authentication success is not evidence of legitimacy where the account itself is compromised.
  • No behavioural baseline on sending patterns. There was no control evaluating whether a financial change request to an internal recipient deviated from the user's normal communication pattern.
  • No payment verification process control. In a production environment, the fraud depends on the recipient acting on emailed banking details without out of band confirmation.

7. Recommendations

# Control What it addresses Cost Priority
1 Out of band payment verification procedure. Mandatory voice confirmation to a previously known number for any change to banking details, regardless of source Breaks the fraud at the point of execution. This is the single control that defeats BEC irrespective of technical detection Process only, no licensing High
2 Alert on inbox rule creation with financial keywords plus hide or delete action Detects the persistence stage. Rule parameters (SubjectContainsWords, MoveToFolder, DeleteMessage) are available in OfficeActivity and are directly queryable $0, analytics rule High
3 Restrict or alert on external mail forwarding rules Closes the exfiltration variant of the same technique, not used in this exercise but standard in real BEC $0, Exchange transport policy High
4 External email tagging Reduces the effectiveness of spoofed sender variants of BEC by making external origin visually explicit $0, included in Exchange Online Medium
5 Behavioural detection on anomalous send patterns (financial vocabulary, unusual recipients, deviation from user baseline) Detects the fraud execution stage where no infrastructure indicator exists $0, analytics rule or UEBA Medium
6 Resolve the underlying identity compromise (token protection, risk based Conditional Access), per INC-ICS-2026-0716-001 Section 7 Removes the access that makes BEC possible $0, included in E5 High

8.2 Social engineering markers present in the fraudulent message

  • Urgency, payment described as due "this week" with confirmation requested "today".
  • Pre emptive unavailability, sender states they are "in meetings", discouraging voice verification.
  • Minimal change request, only the banking details differ from a transaction the recipient already expects to process.
  • Plausible specificity, named invoice reference and amount.

8.3 Data sources used

OfficeActivity for New-InboxRule, Send, MoveToDeletedItems, and SoftDelete. AuditLogs for identity actions. CloudAppEvents is empty in this tenant, so OfficeActivity was used throughout, consistent with the finding recorded in INC-ICS-2026-0716-001.

8.4 Limitations (lab boundaries)

This exercise reproduced the detectable mechanics of BEC, not its financial or multi party dimensions. Excluded honestly from findings:

  • No genuine thread hijacking. There was no real multi message vendor thread to insert into. The fraudulent email was an original send, not a reply within an existing conversation, so Stage 4 is only partially represented.
  • No external vendor or real invoice. All activity was internal to the tenant, with a fabricated invoice reference.
  • No payment, no financial impact, no recovery window. Stage 5 of the kill chain occurs outside M365 and could not be reproduced. Bank recall and law enforcement coordination are therefore untested.
  • No reconnaissance phase in this incident. Mailbox reading was carried out during INC-ICS-2026-0716-001 and was not repeated here.
  • Response timestamps are analyst recorded to the minute, whereas footprint timestamps are taken from audit records and are exact.
